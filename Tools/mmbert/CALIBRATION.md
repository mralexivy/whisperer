# mmBERT editor — precision calibration

**Verdict up front: 0 of 48 cells is enabled. The editor is muted on every language and
every edit class, and it is muted because the model is not precise enough on real speech —
not because the confidence bound is fussy.**

The earlier version of this file (86 held-out real pairs) reported en/punct `.` at
P = 1.0000 and en/case at P = 1.0000 and concluded "good, but the sample is too small to
certify". Recalibrating on **326** held-out real pairs — roughly four times the evidence —
those same cells measure **P = 0.919** and **P = 0.430**. The perfect scores were
small-sample artefacts. That conclusion has been reversed, and this document is the
reversal; anything quoting the old table is quoting a number that did not survive more data.

Generated by `calibrate.py` (`./.venv/bin/python calibrate.py`).
Machine-readable output: `thresholds-calibrated.json`. Full per-split sweeps:
`artifacts/calib_sweeps.json`.

## 1. What was measured

Model: fine-tuned `jhu-clsp/mmBERT-small`, 4 heads (`error` 2-way, `punct` 9-way absolute,
`case` 3-way absolute, `disf` 2-way), checkpoint `artifacts/model/model.pt`,
`keep_bias = 2.0`.

Decision rule (identical in Python and Swift): add `keep_bias = +2.0` to the logit of the
class equal to the token's **current** state → softmax → propose an edit iff
`argmax != current state` **and** `confidence >= threshold`. Binary heads have no current
state; the edit probability is `P(class 1)`.

Precision is measured over **actually applied changes**: of the edits we would make to the
user's text, how many match the target. A word the model rewrites that needed no edit is a
false positive.

170,396 rows, one epoch, 7,099 steps, val loss 0.3033. So the model was trained almost
entirely to restore punctuation deleted from an encyclopedia, and then evaluated entirely on
real speech. That mismatch *is* the gradient in the cross-check table below — wiki 0.993,

corpus, not `mmBERT-small`.** The corpus is being rebuilt from the recordings history alone

and everything below will be re-measured against this same held-out `eval_real_large.jsonl`,
which is pinned so the two runs are comparable.

The shipped `artifacts/thresholds.json` was calibrated on `eval_wiki.jsonl`. **Wiki
thresholds do not transfer** and are used here only as a cross-check.

### Risk tiers — the flat 0.99 rule was wrong and is retired

A flat 99% bar for every edit class was a category error: 99% is the right bar for
*changing what a sentence means*, not for *adding a full stop*. Cells are now judged
against the tier of what the cell can actually do:

| tier | gate | min support | applies to | clean run needed to certify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `meaning` | 0.99 | 300 | error head; punct → NONE (removing a mark) | 299 |
| `disfluency` | 0.97 | 200 | disf head (filler / repetition deletion) | 99 |
| `cosmetic` | 0.95 | 120 | punct insertion (`.` `?` `!` `…` `—`); case LOWER/CAP/UPPER | 59 |

Comma, semicolon and colon insertion are **excluded by construction**, not by sweep:
comma insertion measured P = 0.6720 (41 wrong of 125), and on the held-out golden-set
split the model proposed 17 semicolons and 8 colons where the gold had **zero** of either.
Aggregate `ALL` cells are reported but never enabled — enabling an aggregate would enable
the excluded classes hiding inside it.

A cell is ENABLED at the threshold maximising recall subject to
`Clopper-Pearson one-sided 95% lower bound on precision ≥ tier gate` **and**
`support ≥ tier min`. Grid: 0.30–0.95 step 0.01, 0.95–0.999 step 0.0005, then 0.999…0.99999.

Read the two failure modes separately, because they call for different work:

- **Measured and short** (en/error, en/punct `.`, en/case CAP): enough events to judge, and
  the judgement is that *this checkpoint* is not accurate enough. The earlier revision said
  "more data will not fix these; a better model will" — **retracted.** That inference assumed
  the model had been trained on the 411 real pairs; it had not, and 95.9% of what it did see
  was Wikipedia. Nothing here separates "mmBERT-small is too small" from "the corpus was the
  wrong distribution", and the second explanation is the one the cross-check table supports.
- **Not enough data** (everything Hebrew and Russian, and the rare English marks): 4 Hebrew
  and 10 Russian documents cannot measure anything. These are **unmeasured, not
  measured-and-bad** — do not quote he/disf P = 0.00 as a model property; it is one event.

The gradient is the whole story. Every cell looks best on Wikipedia, worse on
synthetically-corrupted in-domain text, and worst on real ASR output. That ordering is not
noise — it is the corruption generator being the exact inverse of the training objective,
so a synthetic split measures how well the model inverts a function it was trained on
rather than how well it edits speech. **Only the real column may be used to enable a cell.**
Pooling real with synthetic (column 2) inflates every figure and is reported for continuity
with the previous run, not as an authority.

## 4. Plain English — where this leaves the feature

The deterministic passes ship; the model does not.

Punctuation, sentence casing, paragraph breaks, spoken-number conversion, filler removal,
duplicate-word removal and list reflow are all delivered by rule-based passes on the token
graph (`SentenceCaser`, `ParagraphSplitter`, `SpokenNumberConverter`, `TranscriptNormalizer`,
`ListFormatter`), each judged per edit by `ConfidenceGate`, with hard-protected spans that
no pass may touch. Those are measured, fast (p95 ≈ 2.4 ms) and on by default behind the
experimental switch. **None of them depends on this model.**

What the model was supposed to add on top is the judgement calls a rule cannot make —
which specific comma, which mishearing, which sentence boundary in ambiguous prose — and on
326 real transcripts it does not make them well enough to auto-apply. Applying en/error at
its best operating point would rewrite 320 words and get 64 of them wrong. Precision over
recall means that is not a close call.

**What would change the answer**, in the order worth trying:

1. **More real pairs, especially non-English.** 312 English / 4 Hebrew / 10 Russian
   documents is enough to condemn English and not enough to say anything at all about the
   other two. The history database has months of transcripts; the bottleneck is teacher
   labelling, not source material.
2. **A better teacher signal.** The current targets are the 4B's own output, so the model is
   being trained to imitate a teacher that is itself inconsistent on exactly the ambiguous
   cases where the model then fails.
3. **A larger base model or longer training.** `mmBERT-small` at this data scale is the
   cheapest hypothesis to falsify. The earlier revision claimed it *had* been falsified for
   auto-apply — **retracted**, for the reason in the correction at the top of this file. The
   test that would falsify it has not been run yet: one epoch over a 95.9%-Wikipedia corpus,
   with the 411 real pairs excluded by accident, does not test the architecture. The
   history-only retrain is that test.

Until one of those lands, `PolishFeatureFlags.editorKey` stays off, has no Settings UI, and
`ConfidenceGate` refuses every model-sourced edit regardless of the flag. The seam, the
runtime (`MMBERTCoreMLRuntime`, three fixed shapes exported and working), the training
pipeline and this calibration harness all remain in the tree so that a future recalibration
is a re-run rather than a rebuild.
